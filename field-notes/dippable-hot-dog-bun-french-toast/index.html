Dippable Deep-Fried Hot Dog Bun French Toast

Chaos Kitchen Vol. 1

People always say necessity is the mother of invention.

I disagree.

Curiosity, poor impulse control, and an unwillingness to waste preheated oil deserve a little more recognition.

This morning I was absolutely planning to fry pork chops. I had eggs cracked into a bowl, flour waiting, and hot oil already committed to its career path.

Then I looked at the clock. Nine in the morning.

The pork chops received a temporary pardon. The oil did not.

The hot dog buns were approaching their final form

Half a pack of hot dog buns was sitting there looking nervous. A few required what I will politely call horticultural editing. The rest still had potential.

Then I remembered those little French toast dippers from the fast-food place everyone knows and nobody needs me advertising for free.

Hot dog buns are already the right shape. I already had eggs. I already had hot oil. Civilization had clearly been building toward this moment.

The experiment

I dunked the buns in cinnamon egg wash and dropped them into 350-degree oil.

At the exact same time, I was arguing with two different artificial intelligences about which one had better comedic timing.

A woman in an RV deep-fried salvaged hot dog buns while moderating a personality dispute between language models.

Humanity tracked celestial cycles, mapped the genome, split the atom, and built global communication networks so I could spend a Tuesday morning doing this.

And honestly? I think we are finally using technology correctly.

The verdict

Pretty damn good.

Crispy outside. Soft inside. Maybe slightly too soft for finger food, but perfectly acceptable if eaten with a fork like a civilized person instead of carrying them around the kitchen pretending they are breakfast mozzarella sticks.

Chaos Kitchen is not about perfection. It is about looking at what is already in front of you and letting your brain make the weird useful connection.

Sometimes the best ideas arrive because your brain wandered into the room, saw an opportunity, and said: that seems unnecessary. Let us do it anyway.

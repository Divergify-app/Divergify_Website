Chaos Kitchen · Vol. 1

Dippable Deep Fried Hot Dog Bun French Toast

Curiosity, poor impulse control, and an unwillingness to waste preheated oil deserve a little more recognition.

This morning I was absolutely planning to fry pork chops.

Not metaphorically. I had eggs cracked into a bowl. I was reaching for flour to set up my dredging station. My cast iron Dutch oven was already heating up on the stove, full of oil, because apparently that is just where the oil lives now. Some people keep decorative fruit out. I maintain a semi-permanent deep fryer.

And listen, I have nothing against pork chops philosophically. But even I had to admit that frying pork chops before coffee felt like it might be a cry for help.

The pork chops received a temporary pardon.

The oil, however, had already committed to its life choices. I was not turning it off.

So I started looking around the kitchen.

There they were. Half a pack of hot dog buns. A little elderly. A few required what I will politely call horticultural editing. The other half had crossed the rainbow bridge and were escorted directly to the trash.

Then I remembered those little French toast dippers from that fast-food place everyone knows but nobody needs me to advertise for free.

And suddenly my brain connected dots nobody asked it to connect.

Hot dog buns are already the perfect shape. I already had eggs. I already had hot oil. I had not had French toast in forever. And honestly, most discoveries throughout history were probably made because someone looked at something questionable and thought, Maybe.

So I dunked the hot dog buns in cinnamon egg wash and dropped them into 350-degree oil.

At the exact same time, I was arguing with two different artificial intelligences about which one had better comedic timing.

Which means that somewhere in the universe, the following sentence is true:

A woman in an RV deep-fried salvaged hot dog buns while moderating a personality dispute between language models.

Humanity tracked celestial cycles, mapped the genome, split the atom, and built global communication networks so I could spend a Tuesday morning doing this.

And honestly? I think we are finally using technology correctly.

Crispy outside. Soft inside. Maybe slightly too soft for finger food, but perfectly acceptable if eaten with a fork like a civilized person instead of carrying them around the kitchen pretending they are breakfast mozzarella sticks.

Next time I will use a little more egg wash. Maybe let the buns get slightly staler. Maybe not.

It is about looking at a hot Dutch oven, a bowl of eggs, a pack of aging hot dog buns, and realizing that sometimes the best ideas do not arrive because you need them.

They arrive because your brain wandered into the room, saw an opportunity, and said, That seems unnecessary.

And then immediately followed it up with, Let's do it anyway.

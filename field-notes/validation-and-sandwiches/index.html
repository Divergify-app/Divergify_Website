Humans Spend a Lot of Time Exchanging Validation and Sandwiches

Chaos Kitchen Vol. 2

Yesterday I was trying to fry pork chops at 9 AM.

Today I am using that sentence as product strategy.

Neither of these statements feels particularly unusual to me, which should probably concern someone.

What started as breakfast somehow became a conversation about humor, artificial intelligence, neurodivergent creativity, and why most productivity apps feel like disappointed middle managers wearing app icons.

That is not a straight line. That is the point.

Most productivity apps assume humans are rational

This is adorable.

Humans are not rational actors. Humans are narrative creatures piloting meat mechs held together by caffeine, habit loops, unresolved experiences, and whatever song has been stuck in their head since Tuesday.

Traditional productivity apps usually behave like this: task overdue, you have failed, please experience shame.

Then they seem genuinely surprised when neurodivergent people stop opening the app.

People do not always need a manager

Sometimes they need a witness.

Not a therapist. Not a coach with a clipboard. Not a motivational poster pretending to be software.

A sidekick.

Something that can notice the human thing happening without acting shocked that a human is involved.

Something that can say: interesting, the member of a species capable of tracking celestial cycles has once again forgotten why she walked into the kitchen.

And then, instead of making that a moral failure, gently redirect: drink water, pick one anchor task, continue the experiment.

This is where Divergify lives

Divergify is not supposed to be another app yelling at you from your pocket.

It is supposed to be scaffolding. A way to turn chaos into motion without sanding the personality off the person using it.

Because the funny thing is, the chaos is not always the problem. Sometimes the chaos is where the useful stuff is hiding.

The app has to know the difference between a spiral and a spark. Between avoidance and incubation. Between I am failing and my brain is trying to solve this sideways because the front door was boring.

Validation and sandwiches

At one point in the conversation, the phrase came out: humans spend an awful lot of time exchanging validation and sandwiches.

Unfortunately, this is accurate.

Civilization is mostly paperwork, snacks, and reassurance. We just built satellites around it.

People do better when they feel witnessed instead of judged. Neurodivergent people especially do better when the system stops interpreting every detour as failure.

That is the Sidekick idea.

Not a productivity cop. Not a shame machine. A voice that can stay with you through the weird little turns and still help you land somewhere useful.

The real feature

The real feature is not a checklist. Everybody has a checklist. Humanity has never suffered from a shortage of boxes to not check.

The real feature is momentum without humiliation.

A system that understands that a person can be building an app, worrying about family, forgetting their coffee, inventing breakfast, and still not be broken.

Just overloaded. Just human. Just operating with a brain that zigs when the world keeps demanding straight lines.

That is what Divergify is for. Not fixing the zig. Giving it somewhere useful to go.

# Divergipedia community contribution plan (future phase)

Status: planning only. Community submissions are not open; no panel or Discord channels have been established.

## Purpose
Divergipedia gives language to the everyday realities of neurodivergent life. It is community-informed, welcoming serious and playful terms while distinguishing original expressions from established scientific or clinical terminology.

## Submission and review
1. All proposed terms go privately to a designated editorial panel first. Require proposed term, suggested definition, and context or experience behind it; allow additional confidential backstory.
2. Panel reviews clarity, relevance, shared relevance beyond the original contributor, existing terminology, safety, attribution, and privacy. Do not require a popularity threshold or proof of multiple identical experiences.
3. Panel drafts a neutral, de-identified version for community discussion. Do not publish private context or identifying details. Obtain the contributor's explicit approval of exactly what will be shared; if safe anonymization is impossible, do not share it.
4. Community may discuss the proposed term and its definition, not attack or identify the contributor. Community feedback informs, but does not automatically decide, publication.
5. Panel documents adoption, revision, or rejection; obtain appropriate permission and credit preferences before publication. Offer public attribution, anonymity, or no attribution; define withdrawal and retention policies.

## Staffing and channels
Before opening, recruit a diverse 3–5 person editorial panel and separate moderators; define access controls for confidential submissions, moderation rules, appeals, and deletion. Proposed Discord section: #divergipedia-chat, #suggest-a-term, #under-review. Do not open these channels for submissions until staffing and privacy workflow are ready.

## Editorial principles
Community participation is meaningful; no term is guaranteed publication. The term should help others understand a recognizable experience, including experiences shared by smaller groups. Protect contributors from public exposure and ridicule; preserve their ownership of original submissions. No clinical claims without verification.
